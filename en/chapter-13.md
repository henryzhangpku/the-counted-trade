# 13. Beat the Grass to Startle the Snake

*Part III: Stratagems for Attack*

1. The beater strikes the grass, not the snake. The rustle tells him where the snake was, and the snake, startled, tells him more than any map.

2. A paper trade is grass beaten at no price. Nothing is at stake, and the ground still answers.

3. Thirty to fifty forward paper trades on one setup, each recorded before money touches it. Forward, not fitted to the past.

4. The first probe with money is minimum size: the smallest position the market will carry. Size is the one variable that cannot be taken back.

5. A probe that costs a real share of the risk budget is not a probe. It is a position wearing a probe's coat.

6. What the beating reveals is not the snake but the ground: whether the strike fills, how wide the spread sits at the open, whether the move arrives on the day.

7. He who startles the snake and writes nothing has learned only fear. The record is the count, and the count is the point.

8. Do not beat every field. One setup, one patch, until its habits are known.

## Commentary

The stratagem is usually read as a warning: do not alert the enemy. For a buyer of options it is better read as a method. The snake is the market's behaviour around a setup — how it fills, when it moves, what it does to a spread — and the only way to see it is to disturb the grass and watch. The disturbance must be cheap, or it is not observation but commitment.

Paper is the cheapest grass. Thirty to fifty forward trades on one setup, each recorded with its entry, its exit and the reason, will tell the trader more than a year of reading. The number is not magic; it is large enough that a single lucky week does not become a method, and small enough that a working setup is not buried under a decade of waiting.

The first money is a probe at minimum size. If it is wrong, the loss is a rounding error and the information is real. If it is right, the trader has confirmed a fill and a behaviour at the smallest possible rent. What must not happen is the probe growing before the count is complete. A probe that becomes a position has stopped asking questions.
