# 21. The Golden Cicada Sheds Its Shell

*Part IV: Stratagems for Confused Situations*

1. The cicada does not die when it leaves the shell. The husk stays on the branch, whole and empty, and the bird strikes it.

2. A position carried into expiry is that husk. The shape is still on the screen; the life has gone out of it.

3. Leave the spread before the last days, not on them. The final week is where an illiquid short leg is pinned against a strike with nowhere to go.

4. An illiquid short leg held into the pin is no longer a position. It is a place in a queue at the settlement window.

5. Know what settles, and how. Broad index options settle in cash, some at the morning print and some at the close. An ETF option is a claim on shares, and shares can be assigned.

6. The difference is not academic. One expires into cash; the other can expire into a delivery, a margin call, or a short position nobody planned.

7. The trader who writes the exit at entry keeps the choice of the shell. The one who waits for expiry hands that choice to the clearing house.

8. An empty husk is a good disguise for a trader who has already gone.

## Commentary

Expiry is a decision whether or not it is made. A spread left open into the last week is not being held; it is being handed over, and the terms are set by whoever is on the other side of the illiquid leg. The short strike that looked harmless at entry becomes a pin, and the market for closing it becomes a queue.

The settlement distinction is where buyers are most often surprised. Cash-settled index options end as a calculation against an index print; some use the morning print, some the close. ETF options end as claims on shares. A short call there can be assigned before expiry, leaving a short stock position and a margin requirement that were never in the plan. The buyer who does not know which of the two he holds has not finished counting the trade.

None of this requires a forecast. It requires a date, written at entry, on which the spread is closed or rolled, and a habit of checking the contract's settlement terms before the position is opened. The golden cicada is not cleverer than the bird. It is simply gone before the strike.
