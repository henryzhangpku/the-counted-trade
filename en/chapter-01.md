# 1. Cross the Sea Under Camouflage

*Part I: Stratagems for Winning*

1. The emperor feared the sea, so a general built him a house upon the water: painted walls, a threshold, a floor that did not move. He crossed the sea believing he sat on land.

2. So the hope trade enters the account: dressed as conviction, furnished like a plan, and no one aboard asks what water runs beneath the floor.

3. The camouflage is not aimed at the enemy. It is aimed at the passenger. The enemy is the price; the passenger is the buyer.

4. Therefore, before the order, say the five sentences aloud: the edge, the catalyst, the max loss, the time stop, the breakeven win rate.

5. Say them where another can hear, or write them where the later self will read. A sentence that cannot be said is a wall painted on water.

6. The edge is one sentence: what is mispriced, why that is measurable, and what must happen after costs. "I think it goes up" is a wish with a ticket attached.

7. The max loss is the debit, known before the entry and not discovered after it. If the number cannot be spoken, the size was chosen by appetite.

8. Conviction belongs to the rules and to the thesis. It has no business in the size. A size that grows with feeling is the sea showing through the paint.

9. The buyer who cannot say all five is not a buyer of asymmetry. He is a passenger who has paid the fare and believes he is standing on a floor.

10. The sea does not care what the house looks like. At the far shore it asks only whether the floor was real.

## Commentary

The old story has an emperor who would not board a ship, and a general who built him a room on one. The room was real enough to walk in and false in everything that mattered. The hope trade is built to the same plan: a thesis with a grain of truth, a chart that agrees with it, a size that feels like commitment, and no floor under any of it.

The test is cheap, and it is verbal. Five sentences before the order: what is mispriced; which dated event reprices it; what the debit is; on what date the trade is abandoned if the thesis has not confirmed; what win rate the price demands. These are not a ritual. Each one closes a door through which the hope trade would otherwise walk, and the fourth is the one most often left painted shut, because a time stop costs nothing to promise and something to keep.

Conviction, in this book, is a property of rules and thesis, never of size. A trader may be entirely convinced and still take one contract, because the conviction sits in the plan, and the plan already carries the risk. When conviction moves into the size, the camouflage has worked. The passenger is at sea, and believes he is on a floor.
