# 18. To Catch Bandits, Catch Their King

*Part III: Stratagems for Attack*

1. A band of thieves is not broken by chasing every man. Take the chief, and the band loses its head and its plan.

2. Fifteen positions are fifteen bandits. Each is small, each is loud, and none of them is the king.

3. One tested setup is the king. One or two independent theses are his court.

4. Independence is not a label. Two call spreads in the same sector are one thesis wearing two tickers.

5. A lottery ticket is a position that cannot be wrong in any way the holder would notice.

6. Put the risk where the evidence is, not where the count is. A large book of small bets is still one bet on being busy.

7. Test the king: the entry, the exit, the failure. The bandits need no test; they will lose on their own.

8. The account that watches fifteen things watches nothing.

## Commentary

Concentration here is not a matter of one large position. It is a matter of attention and of independent evidence. A buyer can hold several small positions and still be doing one thing, if all of them rise and fall on the same rate decision, the same sector, the same crowded short. The count of positions says nothing; the count of independent theses says everything. Attention is the scarce capital, and it does not diversify.

The king is the setup that has been tested and can be described in the five sentences: edge, catalyst, maximum loss, time stop, breakeven win rate. A court of one or two independent theses is enough to work with. Beyond that, the trader is not diversifying, he is collecting lottery tickets, and each ticket demands the same attention as the king while offering no more evidence.

The test is applied to the king, not to every bandit. A setup is tested once, in paper and then at minimum size, and the result is recorded. The fifteen small positions that were never tested are not a portfolio. They are a way of avoiding the question of whether any of them works. Ask the question once, of the king, and the band is easier to read.
