# 30. Make the Host and the Guest Exchange Roles

*Part V: Stratagems for Gaining Ground*

1. The guest comes in by the side door, stays for supper, and by morning is answering the door as host.

2. In the trade, the guest is the process: the calendar, the five sentences, the size, the time stop, the journal. It arrives as a visitor to one position and must end as the master of the house.

3. The journal records facts, not verdicts. "The stop was moved" is a fact. "The trader is a fool" is a verdict, and a verdict teaches nothing.

4. Count the week: the number of trades, the expectancy after costs, the deepest drawdown, the rules broken.

5. Expectancy is arithmetic, not a feeling: p × average win − (1 − p) × average loss − costs.

6. A broken rule is a datum. Record it beside the profit and loss, not in place of it.

7. The process is itself the first asymmetric bet: downside capped at the risk budget, commitment staged, time stop written before entry.

8. When the process keeps the house, the trade is a guest. When the trade keeps the house, the process has been shown the door.

9. The host need not be right about the guest. He need only know when the guest is to leave.

## Commentary

The stratagem reverses guest and host: the visitor who was tolerated becomes the authority in the house. In a trading account the same reversal is either the whole of the discipline or the whole of the failure. The process begins as something the trader consults — a checklist, a calendar, a size rule — and it either ends by governing the trades or by being ignored whenever a trade is urgent.

The weekly count is where the reversal is made or lost. Four numbers are enough: how many trades, what expectancy after costs, the deepest drawdown, how many rules were broken. Expectancy is p × average win, minus (1 − p) × average loss, minus costs, and p is estimated from the trader's own sample, which is small and uncertain. The formula does not remove the uncertainty; it makes the estimate visible, so that a good week and a good method are not confused.

The broken-rule count is the one most often left out. A week with a profit and three violations is not a good week for the process, whatever the statement says. Written beside the profit and loss, the violations show whether the result came from the method or from the departure. That is the sense in which the pipeline is the first asymmetric bet: its downside is capped by the maximum loss, its commitment is staged, and its time stop is set before there is anything to be impatient about.
