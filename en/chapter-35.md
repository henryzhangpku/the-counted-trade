# 35. Chain Stratagems

*Part VI: Stratagems for Desperate Situations*

1. The ships were lashed together so the fleet would ride steady in the swell. The steadiness was the gift, and one fire was the price.

2. A trade is a chain: the calendar, the five sentences, the size, the alert, the log, the review. One weak link and the rest are cargo.

3. Sunday: the dated events on one calendar, the volatility ranks, the liquidity, five to ten candidates. No more.

4. Before each trade the five sentences are said: edge, catalyst, maximum loss, time stop, breakeven win rate. A missing sentence is a missing link.

5. Size is a link, not an afterthought: the risk budget divided by the maximum loss per unit, rounded down.

6. The alert is a link. The exit is written while the head is cool, not decided while the screen is red.

7. After the trade, the log. Facts, not verdicts.

8. Each week, the review: count, expectancy after costs, maximum drawdown, rule violations.

9. A chain is not a mood. It is a routine that runs in the weeks you do not feel like running it.

10. The buyer's week is the chain, and the chain is what makes the rare winner findable.

## Commentary

The stratagem is usually told as one clever plan nested inside another. Its real point is the linking. No single ring is impressive, and together they hold a fleet. A trading week is built the same way. An hour on Sunday puts the dated events on one calendar, ranks the volatility, checks the liquidity and leaves five to ten candidates; before each entry the five sentences are said aloud; the size is divided down; the alert is set; the log is written; the week is reviewed. None of these is an edge by itself. Together they turn a rare winner into a repeatable process.

The loosest link is usually not analysis but the alert. A trader is calm at entry and is not calm when the screen turns red. An exit written at entry is a plan; an exit invented later is a reaction. The second loosest is the log. "I moved my stop" is a fact and can be counted. "I was an idiot" is a verdict and can only be repeated.

A chain is as strong as its weakest link, which is the whole meaning of the image. A time stop missing from the five sentences leaves the trade with nothing but hope; a size rounded up once undoes every calculation before it. The buyer's time is his enemy, and the chain is his supply line. Cut the line, and no position at the front can be held.
