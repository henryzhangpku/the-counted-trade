# 8. Secretly Cross at Chencang

*Part II: Stratagems for Confronting the Enemy*

1. The plank road into the mountains was burned behind the army, and then rebuilt in plain sight, plank by plank, with the whole world watching the works. The army crossed by Chencang, on a road nobody watched.

2. An earnings date is a plank road. The crowd repairs it for weeks, argues about the number, and on the morning after looks only at the gap.

3. The gap is the noise. The drift is the road, and it begins after the crowd has spent its opinion.

4. Before the print, implied volatility carries the event inside it. After the print, that premium is gone, and the same directional thesis is bought cheaper.

5. Guidance and revisions are the baggage train: they move slowly, in one direction, after the announcement. The drift follows the baggage, not the shout.

6. The road, not the gate: a debit spread two to four weeks out, the near strike bought and the far strike sold, so the event is not paid for twice.

7. Two to four weeks is the length of the road. Shorter, and the drift has not begun; longer, and theta has eaten the fare.

8. The secret is not the direction. It is the date: the second day after the news, not the first.

9. A post-earnings trade without an earnings date is a traveller with another country's map.

10. Before the number, the buyer is one of the archers. After it, he is the courier.

## Commentary

The announcement is the loudest day of the quarter and the least useful to a buyer of direction. The gap reprices the level in a minute. What reprices more slowly is the estimate, the guidance and the positioning around it. That second repricing is the drift, and it takes weeks rather than minutes.

Volatility does the buyer a favour on the way through. Before the print, the option price contains an event premium; after it, the premium collapses. The same two-to-four-week debit spread that was expensive on the day before the number is cheap on the day after. The buyer pays for the road and not again for the gate.

The structure suits a drift because a drift is a bounded move with a date on it. A debit spread caps the payoff, which is a defect only if the thesis is a doubling. Here the thesis is a move of a few per cent over a few weeks, and the short strike is set beyond the move the revisions can plausibly support.

The danger is the gap that has already walked the road. If the print has repriced the year, the drift is in the price before the spread is bought. Drift must be measured in the name, over its own past prints, before it is trusted. A pattern that is famous is also crowded.
