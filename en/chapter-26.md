# 26. Point at the Mulberry, Curse the Locust

*Part V: Stratagems for Gaining Ground*

1. A man points at the mulberry and curses it. The locust on the next branch hears, and mends its ways. The words are aimed at one tree; the correction is meant for another.

2. A trade card speaks in the same figure. It names the evidence and names the absence of evidence; the reader is meant to hear both.

3. "Research, not yet proven, paper only" is not a warning label stuck to the card. It is the card.

4. A label that claims less than the evidence is honest. A label that claims more is a curse uttered at no tree in particular.

5. Say what was tested: which structure, which years, which costs, which exits, which sample.

6. Say what was not: the sample too small, the regime untested, the fill assumed at mid.

7. The seam is where the roof opens. A trader who hides the seam invites the reader to lean on it.

8. A false label is cheap to write and expensive to hold. The trade remembers what the card forgot.

9. Praise the mulberry beyond its fruit, and the locust learns to trust false praise.

10. The tree pointed at must be a real tree. Otherwise the listener learns only to watch the finger.

## Commentary

The stratagem is usually read as discipline by indirection: correct one man by correcting another in his hearing. The trade card works the same way. It is written for the next reader of the position — a partner, a future self, a reviewer — and its honesty is measured by what it declines to claim.

A card that says "pilot, one winner, mean negative" tells the reader how much weight the number can bear. A card that says "edge confirmed" on the same evidence tells him nothing except that the writer wanted it confirmed. The difference is not modesty. It is load-bearing: the label determines whether the result is repeated, reduced or killed.

The trader's own labels matter most when no one else reads them. A journal entry that says "research, paper only" keeps the position in the category where it belongs; an entry that promotes it to "system" before the sample supports it moves real risk onto a beam that has not been tested. An honest label is not a weakness of the card. It is the card.
