# 27. Feign Madness but Keep Your Balance

*Part V: Stratagems for Gaining Ground*

1. The general wears the fool's mask in camp and keeps his accounts by night. The folly is feigned; the balance is real.

2. A day with no qualifying trade looks like a wasted day. It is a position, and often the right one.

3. The funnel admits thousands and returns five. On many days it returns none. The none is the funnel working.

4. To invent a candidate so that the day has a trade is to mistake activity for edge.

5. Feign indifference before the market. Keep the count in private: the five sentences, the calendar, the size.

6. An empty board is not an empty mind. It is a board that has refused every candidate that could not pay.

7. The hand that must act to feel useful will pay the market for the feeling.

8. Folly that believes itself is no longer feigned. A trader who acts the fool until he forgets the count has simply become one.

9. Wait. The market opens again tomorrow, and the count does not expire with the session.

10. To come home empty-handed and keep the measure is skill, not luck.

## Commentary

The stratagem is about a mask worn with a purpose: the appearance of disorder covering a real order. For the buyer the mask is idleness. A session in which nothing passes the funnel looks, from outside, like a session in which nothing was done. From inside it is the last stage of the funnel, where the candidates that survived liquidity and calendar and volatility are rejected for want of an edge.

The funnel from the seventeenth stratagem runs thousands of names down to five or ten. On a quiet day it can run them down to zero. The trader who cannot tolerate zero invents a candidate: a chart that almost qualifies, a catalyst that might arrive, a spread a little too wide. Each of these is a real cost, paid for the comfort of having acted.

The discipline is to record the empty day as a result. The journal should show the candidates reviewed and the reason each was refused; then the empty day is evidence of work rather than absence of it. A board with nothing on it can be the most honest board in the book.
