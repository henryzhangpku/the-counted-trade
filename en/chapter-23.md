# 23. Befriend the Distant, Attack the Near

*Part IV: Stratagems for Confused Situations*

1. The far state is courted with silk and a treaty; the near field is taken acre by acre. The far friend is bought so that the near war may be finished.

2. Five bullish call spreads in five technology names look like five distant states. They answer to one throne.

3. Correlation is the road between them. When one rate, one index, one customer or one fear moves all five, the five are one bet.

4. Count them as one bet, and give that one bet one risk budget, not five.

5. Portfolio heat is what all open trades lose together, not what each loses alone.

6. Keep that heat at two or three per cent of the account. Past it, the fifth spread is not diversification; it is the same bet bought again at a worse price.

7. The distant ally is the trade that shares no catalyst with the rest: another driver, another date, another mechanism.

8. A general who mistakes one enemy for five wins five times on paper and loses once in fact.

## Commentary

The stratagem is usually read as a map of alliances. For the buyer it is a map of exposure. Five call spreads in five names that rise and fall on the same rate, the same index, the same customer or the same liquidity feel like five positions and are one. The screen counts them as five; the account counts them as one when the factor moves.

The arithmetic of heat is where the illusion is paid for. If each spread risks one per cent, five correlated spreads risk five per cent together, and the ceiling for the whole book is two or three per cent. The fifth does not add a new chance; it adds size to a chance already held. In a stress move correlations rise rather than fall, so the diversification that looked real at entry is least real when it is needed.

The distant ally is not a different ticker. It is a different reason: a different catalyst, a different date, a different mechanism, a different sensitivity to the same rate. A book of five names and one reason is a single trade with five commissions. A book of two names and two reasons is two trades.
