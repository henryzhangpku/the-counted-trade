# 34. Inflict Injury on Oneself to Win Trust

*Part VI: Stratagems for Desperate Situations*

1. A general is beaten before his own camp and goes over to the enemy with the wounds still open. He is believed because the wounds are real.

2. The stop is the buyer's wound, taken by his own hand before the enemy can take it for him.

3. A small loss paid at the stated price buys what the market does not sell: the right to remain in the war.

4. The trader who will not bleed a little bleeds all of it, and is believed by no one, least of all himself.

5. Thirty to fifty trades are the only wound that teaches. Below that, the sample is an opinion.

6. Count the hit rate against the breakeven win rate, not against the last good trade.

7. If after the count the setup is not above that line by a comfortable margin, it is killed. A rule is not a mood.

8. A setup you cannot kill is the one that kills you. It will ask for one more trade, and then one more.

9. The kill rule is written while the setup is still beautiful. Written afterwards, it is a eulogy.

10. The buyer's edge is rare and large. A low hit rate is not a shame; a setup that never clears its line is a habit.

## Commentary

The first injury is the stop. It is taken voluntarily, at a price written before the entry, and its purpose is not to please the market but to keep the account's word intact. A trader who has paid a stop at the stated price knows something about himself that no winning trade teaches. A trader who has widened it once knows something worse.

The second is the count. After thirty to fifty trades a setup has a hit rate, and a hit rate means nothing until it is set against the breakeven win rate. On a debit spread the breakeven win rate is the debit divided by the width of the strikes, so the comparison is arithmetic and can be made without an opinion. Compare the two numbers, and leave room for the costs. A hit rate that only just clears the line is a hit rate that does not clear it.

The third injury is the kill. A setup traded for a year becomes part of the trader's idea of himself, and killing it feels like admitting the year was wasted. So it is granted one more trade. The rule is written at the beginning, when the setup is still a hypothesis and not an identity. A method that cannot be killed is not a method. It is a habit with a story attached.
